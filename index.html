Permanent law

The first-year write-off became permanent federal law in 2025, for equipment acquired after January 19, 2025. No phase-out to plan around.

Year-end deadline

The clinic equipment has to be treating patients by December 31 for the deduction to count this year. We document every step.

Financing counts

Financed dollars count toward the deduction, so a down payment of around 12.5% can support the whole deduction.

New or used

Used equipment qualifies when it's your first use and it's acquired arm's-length from an unrelated seller. Recapture under §1245 applies on a later sale.

The passive trap

A bare equipment lease

Hand over a machine, collect a check, provide no services. That's per se a rental under §469: the year-one loss suspends instead of offsetting practice income or wages, and §179 is barred for noncorporate lessors. The deduction is real, but stranded.

How we build it

Equipment plus significant services

When real services come with the equipment and you materially participate, the activity leaves the rental box, so the loss can offset other active income.

  • Staffs the clinical team. Nurses and technicians on your entity's payroll or service contracts.
  • Manages utilization. Scheduling, throughput, uptime and maintenance, with reporting that proves it.
  • Performs the billing. Your entity bills every treatment and runs collections.
  • Documents participation. Contemporaneous time logs, service agreements and utilization records.
§469

Passive activity rules

The services layer moves the activity out of the rental box. You then materially participate under one of the seven tests, with documentation.

§465

At-risk rules

Deductions are limited to amounts genuinely at risk. Cash or recourse debt from unrelated lenders. No nonrecourse seller paper, no guaranteed-return side letters.

§461(l)

Excess business loss cap

For 2026, losses offset nonbusiness income up to $256,000 single or $512,000 joint. The rest carries forward as an NOL. We model the cap before sizing any acquisition.

Straight answers.

Including when this isn't the right move for you.

Is this a tax shelter?

No, and the distinction matters. Marketed deals where passive investors acquire equipment chiefly to harvest depreciation are an IRS enforcement focus. This is an operating business: equipment acquired at retail market price in an arm's-length transaction, real staffing and billing, recourse financing, and a credible pre-tax profit objective. The tax treatment follows the economics, not the other way around.

Do I have to be involved day to day?

The strategy is built for people who materially participate, typically 100 to 500+ hours a year depending on which §469 test applies, with contemporaneous time logs. If you can't participate, expect passive treatment, which changes how the losses can be used. We cover this candidly in every consultation.

How do I materially participate?

To use the deduction against wages or business income, you have to materially participate in the clinic under one of the IRS's seven tests — in practice a real, regular and continuous role, generally 100 to 500+ hours a year depending on the test, documented with contemporaneous time logs. Owning the clinic's marketing and demand generation can be a genuine part of that role, and we help you build it and document it — much of it from the desktop — but it has to be real work and real hours, enough to clear the test, not a box to tick. We walk through exactly what that looks like for your facts on the first call.

What equipment qualifies for 100% bonus depreciation?

Medical equipment is generally 5-year MACRS property, well within §168(k)'s 20-year ceiling. Both the binding acquisition contract and the placed-in-service date must fall after January 19, 2025. Used or refurbished equipment qualifies if it's your first use and acquired arm's-length from an unrelated party.

What happens when the equipment is sold?

Bonus depreciation takes basis to zero, so gain on sale up to original cost is recaptured as ordinary income under §1245. The deduction is a deferral with real time-value benefit, not a permanent exclusion, and exit planning is part of every engagement.

Are there healthcare regulatory issues?

Potentially, yes. Where referring physicians hold an interest, the Stark Law and Anti-Kickback Statute constrain per-use and revenue-share compensation. Every placement involving referral relationships is reviewed by healthcare regulatory counsel before signing.
