## What You're Actually Building: The Cash-Pay Biohacking Clinic Model

The structure is an operating wellness clinic delivering advanced therapies, plasmapheresis, EBOO, TBI protocols, IV nutrient therapy, and related longevity modalities, to patients who pay at the point of service. No insurance billing. No prior authorizations. No fee schedule compression.

### Why Cash-Pay Changes the Revenue Math

Insurance reimbursement caps what a provider earns per procedure and when they get paid. Cash-pay removes both constraints. Therapies like EBOO (Extracorporeal Blood Oxygenation and Ozonation) and plasmapheresis command per-session fees well above the cost of a single treatment cycle, creating high-margin revenue streams outside insurance reimbursement structures entirely. When you control pricing and collect at time of service, the margin profile is stronger and the revenue model is cleaner. That's the foundation of the [cash-pay wellness equipment business model](https://mddeductions.com/blog/cash-pay-wellness-medical-equipment-business-model) that makes the equipment investment viable.

### The Operating Business Requirement You Can't Skip

The depreciation deduction exists because you own equipment used in an active trade or business. That business must be real, licensed, staffed, patient-facing, and generating revenue. A holding company that leases equipment to a shell is not this. A clinic with a medical director, nursing staff, a patient schedule, and monthly revenue is. The IRS does not reward structure without substance, and any advisor who tells you otherwise is understating the audit exposure.

## Biohacking Wellness Clinic Startup Equipment: What to Buy and Why It Matters for Depreciation

Equipment selection in a longevity medicine clinic setup is both a clinical and a tax decision. What you buy determines what you treat; it also determines what you depreciate, on what schedule, and whether bonus depreciation applies at all.

### Core Therapy Equipment: Plasmapheresis, EBOO, and TBI Protocols

Three equipment categories anchor most biohacking clinic builds in 2026:

- **Plasmapheresis systems**, apheresis machines that separate and filter plasma, used in longevity and autoimmune-adjacent protocols. See the [Plasmapheresis equipment tax deduction breakdown](https://mddeductions.com/blog/plasmapheresis-business-tax-deduction-guide) for unit-level classification detail. These systems depreciate as 5-year MACRS property under the general asset class for medical equipment.

- **EBOO therapy systems**, extracorporeal ozone treatment units that oxygenate and re-infuse blood. The [EBOO therapy equipment deduction guide](https://mddeductions.com/blog/eboo-therapy-equipment-business-deduction) covers sourcing and classification. EBOO units generally qualify as 5-year MACRS property and are eligible for §168(k) bonus depreciation when new or new-to-the-taxpayer.

- **TBI protocol equipment**, neurostimulation devices, hyperbaric-adjacent systems, and photobiomodulation units used in traumatic brain injury recovery protocols. TBI therapy is an emerging cash-pay niche where patient demand consistently outpaces local provider availability, a supply gap that favors new clinic entrants. Costs and classification details are in the [TBI therapy equipment costs for cash-pay clinics](https://mddeductions.com/blog/tbi-therapy-equipment-cost-cash-pay-clinic) breakdown.

### Equipment Sourcing, Useful Life, and MACRS Classification

Most clinical equipment in a biohacking clinic falls under 5-year MACRS property (IRS Asset Class 57.0, distributive trades and services, or Class 00.12 for information systems) or 7-year MACRS where the asset doesn't fit a specific class. The distinction matters because MACRS class life determines the depreciation schedule without bonus depreciation, and confirms eligibility for bonus treatment with it.

Sourcing new equipment from an authorized distributor creates a clean paper trail: invoice, placed-in-service date, and manufacturer documentation of useful life. All three are useful if the deduction is ever examined. The [MACRS depreciation schedules for medical equipment](https://mddeductions.com/blog/medical-equipment-depreciation-schedule-macrs) guide maps asset classes to common clinic equipment categories.

## Startup Cost Structure for an Alternative Wellness Center Equipment Investment

A realistic longevity clinic build in 2026 draws from four cost buckets. Knowing which bucket each dollar falls into tells you what hits the depreciation schedule versus what runs through P&L in year one.

### Capital Expenditures vs. Operating Costs: Know the Difference

**Capital expenditures (depreciation schedule):**
- Medical equipment (plasmapheresis, EBOO, TBI systems, IV infusion stations): $300,000–$700,000 depending on modality mix and unit count
- Leasehold improvements: built-in millwork, plumbing, medical-grade electrical, these depreciate over 15 years as qualified improvement property (QIP), also eligible for bonus depreciation
- Furniture and fixtures: 7-year MACRS

**Operating costs (run through P&L):**
- Staffing: licensed nurses, medical technicians, front desk
- Supplies: disposables, IV consumables, ozone-grade tubing
- Licensing and credentialing fees
- Marketing and lead generation

Equipment is the lever. A $500,000 equipment package placed in service in year one generates a $500,000 deduction under current §168(k) rules, dollar for dollar against active income. Operating costs deliver deductions too, but they recur annually and don't compress tax liability with the same front-loaded force.

## Cash-Pay Clinic Depreciation Deduction: The §168(k) Bonus Depreciation Play

This is the mechanism that makes the biohacking clinic business medical equipment investment attractive to high-income owners and investors beyond the clinic economics alone.

### 100% Year-One Write-Off: Mechanics of the Deduction

Under IRC §168(k), qualifying property placed in service in 2026 is eligible for 100% bonus depreciation, a full first-year deduction equal to the asset's cost basis. The phase-down that reduced bonus to 60% in 2024 and 80% in 2025 was legislatively extended; qualifying equipment placed in service in 2026 can be fully expensed in year one. A $500,000 equipment package generates a deduction of that same magnitude against active income in the first tax year.

The deduction offsets active income, W-2 wages, business income, professional income, not passive investment returns. That distinction is governed by IRC §469. An owner who materially participates in the clinic's operations meets the active income standard; one who writes a check and shows up quarterly does not. Material participation under Treas. Reg. §1.469-5T requires satisfying at least one of seven tests, most commonly, participating more than 500 hours per year or more than any other individual. The mechanics of [§168(k) bonus depreciation for active medical equipment businesses](https://mddeductions.com/blog/active-income-medical-equipment-business-168k-bonus-depreciation) and what "active" actually requires are worth reviewing before you structure the entity.

The [passive activity loss rules under IRC §469 for medical equipment](https://mddeductions.com/blog/passive-activity-loss-medical-equipment-irc-469) create real risk for investors who take a hands-off approach. If the IRS classifies your participation as passive, the depreciation loss is suspended, deferred until you generate passive income or dispose of the activity.

### §179 as a Secondary Tool and Its 2026 Limits

IRC §179 lets a business elect to expense qualifying property in the year it's placed in service, subject to a dollar cap and a taxable income limitation. The §179 deduction limit for 2026 is $1,220,000 (indexed for inflation), with a phase-out beginning at $3,050,000 of total property placed in service. The taxable income limitation means §179 cannot create a loss, it can only reduce income to zero.

§168(k) carries no such income limitation. It can and does create a net loss that offsets income from other sources. For a high-income investor in the 37% bracket deploying $500,000 into qualifying equipment, the year-one tax saving from bonus depreciation alone can approach $185,000. §179 works as a complement, for property that may not qualify for bonus depreciation, or to fine-tune the deduction, but it is not a substitute for §168(k) when the goal is full year-one offset.

## Startup Medical Equipment Write-Off Strategy: Staffing and Compliance from Day One

The deduction is defensible only when the business is operational. "Operational" has a specific meaning: licensed clinicians treating patients, documented treatment protocols, proper billing records, and an owner who can demonstrate their own participation.

### Hiring Licensed Clinicians: Who You Need and When

A functional biohacking clinic requires:

- **Registered Nurses (RNs) or Licensed Practical Nurses (LPNs):** Required for IV administration, apheresis monitoring, and most infusion protocols in every state. Hire before you open.
- **Medical Director:** A licensed physician or, in some states, an NP/PA operating under physician oversight. Required for clinical supervision, protocol sign-off, and regulatory compliance.
- **Medical Technicians:** For EBOO and plasmapheresis equipment operation, depending on state scope-of-practice rules.
- **Front Office / Billing Staff:** Patient intake, consent documentation, cash payment processing, and records management. These roles also generate the operational paper trail that substantiates material participation.

Staffing is not optional compliance overhead. It is the operational substance that distinguishes an active medical equipment business from a passive investment arrangement, and it is what the IRS examines when evaluating whether depreciation losses are properly characterized as active or passive.
